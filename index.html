01 / About

Scope of work

The brain is one of the most lipid-rich organs in the human body — yet how lipids are acquired, distributed, and metabolized in the central nervous system remains poorly understood. Our lab works at the interface of chemistry, biochemistry, and neuroscience to fill that gap.

We integrate LC‑MS/MS lipidomics, metabolic tracing, chemical proteomics, organic synthesis, genetics, biochemistry, and iPSC‑derived and murine models to ask how deregulated lipid metabolism drives neurodegeneration.

03 / Research

Five questions we're chasing

Our long-running program is structured around five fundamental, interconnected questions about lipid biology in the central nervous system.

01

How much of the brain's lipid is dietary vs. locally synthesized?

The blood-brain barrier restricts movement of many circulating lipids, so neural cells must tightly regulate lipid uptake and endogenous synthesis to maintain membrane integrity, myelination, and signaling. We seek to understand how the brain balances these distinct sources — and how disruption of these pathways contributes to neurological disease.

Blood‑brain barrier Cholesterol efflux ApoE pathways Glymphatic clearance
02

How does the brain split glucose between energy and lipid synthesis?

Glucose is the primary energy source for the brain — but it's also a critical building block for the synthesis of complex lipids, including glycosphingolipids that are essential for neuronal membranes, myelin formation, and cellular signaling. How the brain allocates glucose between these competing demands remains poorly understood.

Metabolic tracing Glycosphingolipids Mitochondria Myelin
03

How do neurons & glia coordinate lipid metabolism?

Neurons have limited capacity for lipid storage and synthesis, so they rely heavily on glial partners — astrocytes and oligodendrocytes for lipid synthesis, microglia for lipid recycling. We're working to understand how these cell types coordinate trafficking, utilization, and clearance to maintain neuronal function and myelin integrity, and how breakdown of this partnership feeds lipid accumulation, neuroinflammation, and neurodegeneration.

Astrocytes Oligodendrocytes Microglia Cell‑cell trafficking
04

How does genetic variation in lipid metabolism predispose to neurodegeneration?

Mutations in genes involved in cholesterol metabolism, sphingolipid degradation, and lipid transport can disrupt neuronal lipid balance — leading to lipid accumulation, neuroinflammation, and cellular dysfunction. We work to understand why these defects selectively predispose neurons to degeneration, with the goal of uncovering new biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

APOE · ABCA GBA · GRN ABHD12 · SPTLC1 Lysosomal homeostasis
05

Can lipid signatures predict sporadic neurodegeneration before symptoms?

Most neurodegenerative diseases occur sporadically, without a clear inherited cause — yet many exhibit profound disturbances in lipid metabolism. Aging, diet, metabolic stress, inflammation, and environmental factors may progressively disrupt lipid homeostasis in the brain. We're working to understand how these alterations emerge during early disease progression, and whether specific lipid signatures can serve as biomarkers to predict risk before symptoms appear.

Plasma lipidomics Biomarkers Aging Lipid droplets
04 / Approach

An interdisciplinary toolkit

Lipid biology demands tools from many fields. We combine quantitative mass spectrometry, chemical biology, and modern cellular models to ask questions no single technique could answer.

01

LC‑MS/MS lipidomics

Quantitative profiling of hundreds of lipid species across tissues, fluids, and cellular compartments.

02

Metabolic tracing

Isotope-labeled precursors track the synthesis, breakdown, and inter-organelle flow of brain lipids.

03

Chemical proteomics

Activity-based probes and chemical-genetic screens map lipid-binding and lipid-modifying enzymes.

04

Organic synthesis

Custom lipid probes, isotopologs, and bioactive analogs designed and synthesized in-house.

05

Genetics & molecular biology

CRISPR, knockouts, and disease-variant models to interrogate gene–lipid–phenotype links.

06

iPSC‑derived models

Patient-derived stem cells differentiated into neurons, astrocytes, and microglia for human disease modeling.

07

Murine models

Genetically engineered mouse models for in-vivo lipidomics, behavior, and longitudinal disease tracking.

08

Biochemistry

Enzymology and structural biochemistry of lipid hydrolases, transferases, and transport proteins.
